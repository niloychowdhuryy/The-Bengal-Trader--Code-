# Historical Events and Place Notes

## Chittagong campaign and capture, 1665–1666

**Status:** Documented political and military history; the game's route and price modifiers are fictional.  
**Place:** Chittagong (modern Chattogram), the Karnaphuli and the coast.  
**Game context:** Dhaka, c. 1665.

### Documented account

Md. Abdul Karim's *Banglapedia* article “Shaista Khan” reports that Shaista Khan became Bengal's subahdar in 1663; a campaign against Chittagong was sent in the final week of December 1665; a joint land and naval expedition fought around the Karnaphuli; and the Chittagong fort was captured on **26 January 1666**. Mughal administration later renamed the city Islamabad.

**Primary reference for the detailed chronology:** Md. Abdul Karim, “Shaista Khan,” *Banglapedia: National Encyclopedia of Bangladesh*.  
https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Shaista_Khan

**Independent cross-check:** “Chittagong,” *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, which confirms the 1666 occupation by a force dispatched by Shaista Khan; it does not give the precise January day. The exact date above is attributed to Banglapedia, not to both references.  
https://www.britannica.com/place/Chittagong

### What the game adds

The opening 36 in-game days are an approximate story window, not a calendar conversion. During this window, the Chittagong route can add **up to one in-game day** (one real-world minute), subject to the five-minute maximum for any voyage, and both salt quotes at Chittagong rise by **six gold**. These are fictional market/travel rules. Neither source documents those prices, a port closure or a river blockade. A separate fictional hired river raider teaches the encounter system; its character, stats, outcome and theft rules are invented.

The game does not portray a whole community as hostile. The historical account concerns particular political and military actors and a specific campaign.

## Dhaka and the riverside city prototype

*Banglapedia*'s “Dhaka” describes Dhaka as a Mughal provincial capital from 1610, linked by the Buriganga and Dhaleswari to Bengal's wider river system. It records that Niccolao Manucci visited in 1663 and described many houses as straw-built; it also cites Jean-Baptiste Tavernier's 1666 observation that residents wanted to live beside the river.

**Reference:** “Dhaka,” *Banglapedia: National Encyclopedia of Bangladesh*.  
https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Dhaka

The city visual prototype is a generic illustrated settlement inspired by that river-facing context. Its clickable market, docks, mosque, home, warehouse, town centre, workshop and farm are gameplay affordances, **not claims that these exact buildings, layouts or named structures are documented at each port**. Dhaka's article notes pre-Mughal mosque remains, but no particular mosque in the prototype is a reconstruction. The new riverside layout, building forms, boats and street pattern are original interpretation, not a surveyed or documented 1665 city plan.

The Banglapedia “Architecture” entry discusses building materials and forms across many periods and regions; it does not establish that each material shown in this prototype was used in Dhaka in 1665. The scene therefore combines visual interpretation for readability and play, and should not be used as evidence for a particular historical building or neighbourhood.

*Banglapedia*'s “Sonargaon” notes that after the Mughal defeat of Musa Khan in 1611 the town continued as the headquarters of a Bengal *sarkar* while Dhaka was the Mughal capital. The current map and economy do not model that administrative history yet.

**Reference:** “Sonargaon,” *Banglapedia: National Encyclopedia of Bangladesh*.  
https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Sonargaon

## River-network accuracy limits

The five-port river graph, channel bends, travel days, abstract minimum depths and vessel-draft checks are **gameplay abstractions**. They are not sourced 17th-century hydrographic measurements or reliable reconstructions of historic navigability. Use mapped game routes for play only. Do not present them as historically accurate sailing directions.

## Future event records

Before adding an event, record:

- The dated historical claim, place, named actors and reliable sources, noting uncertainty or variants.
- What is documented versus what is invented for play.
- The affected ports, waterways, infrastructure, local stock, trade restrictions and interval.
- The player warning, available response or recovery, and event end condition.

Possible effect types include temporary route-edge closure, military control, port restriction, damaged infrastructure, interrupted shipments and delayed production. The navigation model accepts event, military and weather blockage inputs, but **no current segment closure is active**. The Chittagong campaign alert is intentionally a delay and salt-quote modifier, not a historical blockade.

Never infer hostility, motive or behavior for an entire ethnic, religious or cultural community from a specific political conflict.
