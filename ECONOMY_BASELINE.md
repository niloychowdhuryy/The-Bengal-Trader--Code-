## What's still out of balance

These are recorded for later work and weren't redesigned:

- **Farms** still give free rice, and merchants don't compete with it. Farms are now the strongest strategy by far.
- **Automated ships:** measured after Step 3, a Bora earned 342 Coins a day, less than manual trading's 511, and a Patello earned 680. Current figures are in the tables above.
- **Contracts:** only 1 of 15 delivery contracts pays more than its goods, and rewards come mostly early, which is why day-30 mixed wealth misses its target.
- **Warehouses** roughly break even on the route measured.
- **Merchants' wealth** grows about 1.7× in 200 days, below the 3–8× first target. It's a deliberate result of the margins above: a faster-growing world left the player no room. The target should be reviewed with the leaderboards.
- **Money** still comes from the market without limit. Prices can't inflate because they depend on stock, not on how much money exists, but wealth has no limit other than stock recovery.

# Gold-era record (save version 20)

The game's economy as it stood before Coins. All amounts in this record are in **gold**, the currency at the time. Multiply by 50 to compare with Coins.

## What stands out

1. **Money can be made from nothing.** Buying and reselling at one port earns money once a stall's stock is low, because a sale is priced from the stock level after the purchase. 92 of 120 stalls can reach this, 20 of them with one 12-unit hold. A new game reached it through normal commands: on day 85.39, with 504 gold, it bought 10 lac at satgaon, then earned 200 gold in 200 loops with 0 days passing. Nothing limits the number of loops.
2. **Farms are the dominant strategy.** The farm player ends on 4758 net worth against 1833 for manual trading, because farm rice is free. One farm repays itself in about 40 days and keeps paying.
3. **Automated ships out-earn hand trading, for a small purchase.** A Bora left on a route earns 7.88 gold a day and repays itself in 44 days, while manual trading earned 6.45 a day. A River boat earns the same on routes and costs more.
4. **The market barely moves.** The price index stays at 1 after 200 days, and stalls are on average 0.03 units from their starting stock. No stall ran near empty or full in any strategy's median run.
5. **NPC merchants hardly matter.** 3 of 4 had stopped trading by day 150, and together they move 2.26 units a day. They lost 437 gold of income to the purse limit (estimated).
6. **A warehouse costs more than it earns.** On the same route, warehouses end on 1530 against 1982 without them, since prices recover the same whether or not goods are stored.
7. **Most delivery contracts lose money.** Only 1 of 15 delivery contracts pay more than the goods handed over.
8. **First trips look far more unequal than routes really are.** The best first trip earns 20.1× a top-tenth route, but a stall can't sustain that. Run repeatedly, the best route is 1.9× a top-tenth route.

## What this can't tell us

- **City unlocks, seasons, weather, political events, pirates and voyage choices** don't exist yet, so their effect and pacing can't be measured. The only encounter is the Chittagong introduction.
- **Inflation** in the usual sense can't appear: prices depend only on stock, never on how much money exists. The market pays out without limit, which is the real risk.
- **Scripted players aren't people.** They're competent, not optimal, and a skilled player may do better. They can't show what's fun, clear or tedious.
- **Activity levels** only change real time, since closed time counts for nothing. Casual and active players differ here in judgement and speed, not in what the world does.
- **Farm-heavy play** is limited to 6 farms and to the ports the bot happens to pass. All 15 weren't tried.
- **Warehouses** were tested on one route with one simple rule. A cleverer plan might do better.
- **Real phones:** none of this says anything about performance on a device.
